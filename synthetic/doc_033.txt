REASON FOR ENCOUNTER: Annual comprehensive geriatric assessment and fall risk evaluation.

SUBJECTIVE:
Patient is an 82-year-old female living alone in a single-story home, accompanied by her daughter. Her daughter reports that the patient had two non-injurious ground-level falls in the last 4 months (one tripping on a throw rug, another when getting out of bed quickly at night).   * Timed Up and Go (TUG) Test: 16.2 seconds (>12 seconds indicates high fall risk).
  * 4-Stage Balance Test: Unable to hold tandem stance beyond 4 seconds.

ASSESSMENT & PLAN:
Multifactorial fall risk and orthostatic hypotension exacerbated by polypharmacy and Beers Criteria medications (Zolpidem, Diphenhydramine).
1. Deprescribing:
   * Discontinue Diphenhydramine completely.
   * Structured taper plan for Zolpidem (reduce to 5 mg for two weeks, then discontinue) with cognitive-behavioral sleep hygiene education.
   * Reduce Hydrochlorothiazide to 12.5 mg daily to mitigate postural blood pressure drops.
2. Physical Therapy: Referral for outpatient PT focusing on lower extremity strengthening, gait balance training, and cane fitting.
3. Home Safety: Ordered home occupational therapy evaluation for environmental modifications (removal of throw rugs, installation of bathroom grab bars, nightlights).
4. Bone Health: Check 25-OH Vitamin D level; initiate Vitamin D3 1000 IU daily.
