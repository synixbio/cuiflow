ADMISSION DIAGNOSIS: Status Asthmaticus.
DISCHARGE DIAGNOSES:
1. Acute severe asthma exacerbation, resolved.
2. Moderate persistent asthma.
3. Allergic rhinitis.

HOSPITAL COURSE:
A 7-year-old male with known asthma presented to the pediatric ED with severe acute dyspnea, tachypnea (RR 48 bpm), and subcostal retractions following a viral upper respiratory infection. Room air SpO2 was 89%. He remained in moderate-to-severe respiratory distress after 3 back-to-back nebulized Albuterol/Ipratropium treatments and was transferred to the inpatient pediatric unit on continuous Albuterol nebulization (10 mg/hr) and IV Methylprednisolone 1 mg/kg Q12H.

Over the next 36 hours, his bronchospasm improved markedly. He was transitioned from continuous nebulizers to intermittent Albuterol treatments spaced from every 2 hours to every 4 hours. Systemic steroids were transitioned to oral Prednisolone 1 mg/kg/day. At the time of discharge, his lungs are completely clear without wheezing, work of breathing is normal, SpO2 is 98% on room air, and peak expiratory flow is 85% of his personal best.

DISCHARGE MEDICATIONS:
1. Prednisolone oral suspension 20 mg PO daily for 3 more days (complete 5-day course; no taper needed).
2. Fluticasone propionate / Salmeterol (Advair Diskus 100/50 mcg) 1 puff BID (daily controller).
3. Albuterol HFA 90 mcg inhaler: 2 puffs with valved holding chamber Q4H PRN cough or wheezing.
4. Cetirizine 5 mg PO daily for allergic rhinitis.

FOLLOW-UP PLAN:
* Pediatrician follow-up in 3-5 days.
* Updated Asthma Action Plan provided and reviewed with parents.
