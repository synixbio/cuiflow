PATIENT: Synthetic-040 | AGE: 8 months | SEX: M | ADMISSION: 2026-12-18 | DISCHARGE: 2026-12-22
ADMISSION DIAGNOSIS: Acute viral bronchiolitis with respiratory distress and poor feeding.
DISCHARGE DIAGNOSES:
1. Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV) bronchiolitis, resolved.
2. Dehydration secondary to respiratory distress, resolved.

HOSPITAL COURSE:
An 8-month-old male infant presented with a 3-day history of clear rhinorrhea, cough, and fevers, progressing to rapid breathing, subcostal retractions, and poor feeding (wet diapers decreased to 2 per 24 hours). Nasopharyngeal PCR on admission was positive for Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV). Initial room air oxygen saturation was 88%, with respiratory rate of 62 bpm.

Patient was admitted to the pediatric ward and placed on high-flow nasal cannula (HFNC) support at 8 L/min with FiO2 30%, alongside IV maintenance fluids (D5 0.45% NS with 20 mEq KCl). Frequent nasal suctioning was performed prior to small volume bottle-feeding attempts. Over the next 72 hours, work of breathing improved steadily. HFNC was weaned to standard low-flow nasal cannula on Day 3 and discontinued on Day 4. Patient resumed ad libitum formula feeding, achieving normal oral hydration status. At discharge, infant is breathing comfortably on room air with normal feeding and 5 wet diapers in the preceding 18 hours.

DISCHARGE INSTRUCTIONS:
* Normal saline nasal drops (2-3 drops per nostril) followed by bulb suctioning before feedings and naps.
* Continue frequent small-volume oral formula feeds.
* Avoid second-hand tobacco smoke exposure.
* Follow up with pediatrician Dr. Jennifer Adams in 48 to 72 hours.
* Return precautions reviewed: worsening retractions, grunting, lethargy, or poor feeding.
