REASON FOR ENCOUNTER: Evaluation of exertional lightheadedness and systolic murmur.

SUBJECTIVE:
The patient is a 74-year-old male with hypertension and hyperlipidemia who reports progressive exertional dyspnea and lightheadedness when walking up an incline or climbing stairs over the past 5 months. He has had two near-syncopal episodes where he felt the world "graying out" after mowing the lawn, resolving promptly upon sitting down. Denies true syncope, orthopnea, chest pressure, or paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea.

OBJECTIVE:
Vitals: BP 106/78 mmHg (narrow pulse pressure), HR 66 bpm regular, SpO2 96% room air.
Physical Exam:
* Carotid Arteries: Slow upstroke with low-amplitude pulses bilaterally (pulsus parvus et tardus).
* Cardiovascular: Sustained apical impulse. S2 is soft and single. A harsh, late-peaking 3/6 crescendo-decrescendo systolic ejection murmur is best heard at the right second intercostal space, radiating prominently to bilateral carotids. Murmur decreases with Valsalva maneuver.
* Lungs: Clear to auscultation bilaterally.
* Extremities: No edema.

DIAGNOSTIC STUDIES:
* Transthoracic Echocardiogram: Calcified, heavily stenotic trileaflet aortic valve.
  * Aortic Valve Area (AVA): 0.72 cm^2 (Severe: < 1.0 cm^2).
  * Mean Transvalvular Gradient: 48 mmHg (Severe: > 40 mmHg).
  * Peak Jet Velocity: 4.4 m/s (Severe: > 4.0 m/s).
  * LVEF: 52% with concentric left ventricular hypertrophy.

ASSESSMENT & PLAN:
Severe symptomatic aortic valve stenosis (Stage D).
1. Referral to Structural Heart Multidisciplinary Team: Given age (74 years) and classic symptoms, patient is a candidate for Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement (TAVR) vs. Surgical AVR.
2. Diagnostics Ordered: ECG-gated Cardiac CT Angiography (TAVR protocol) of chest, abdomen, and pelvis to assess transfemoral vascular access and annular dimensions.
3. Activity Guidance: Strictly avoid strenuous physical exertion or heavy lifting until valve replacement is completed.
4. Medication Adjustments: Avoid aggressive vasodilators or high-dose nitrates which could precipitate hemodynamic collapse.
