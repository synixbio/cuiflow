DISCHARGE DIAGNOSES:
1. Diabetic ketoacidosis, resolved.

HOSPITAL COURSE:
A 23-year-old female with an 8-year history of Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus presented with nausea, recurrent vomiting, abdominal pain, and Kussmaul respirations. Her personal continuous glucose monitor had been malfunctioning for 2 days, and she had omitted multiple basal insulin doses due to financial barriers in obtaining glargine refills. Admission labs: Blood glucose 542 mg/dL, arterial blood gas pH 7.18, bicarbonate 9 mEq/L, anion gap 28, beta-hydroxybutyrate 6.8 mmol/L, serum potassium 5.2 mEq/L.

Patient was admitted to the step-down unit and managed on the institutional DKA insulin protocol. Aggressive fluid resuscitation with isotonic saline was initiated, transitioning to D5 0.45% NS once blood glucose reached 220 mg/dL. Potassium repletion was added to IV fluids. By Hospital Day 2 (26 hours), the anion gap closed to 10, bicarbonate rose to 20 mEq/L, and she was transitioned to subcutaneous insulin with a 2-hour overlap before the insulin drip was discontinued. Certified diabetes educators met with the patient and enrolled her in pharmaceutical manufacturer co-pay assistance.

DISCHARGE MEDICATIONS:
1. Insulin Glargine 24 units subcutaneous once daily at bedtime.
2. Insulin Lispro 5 units subcutaneous before meals, plus correction scale.
3. Ketone urine test strips.
4. Dexcom G7 Continuous Glucose Monitoring supply kit.

DISCHARGE PLAN:
