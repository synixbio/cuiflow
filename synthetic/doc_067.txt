DISCHARGE DIAGNOSES:
1. Acute ischemic infarct of the right frontoparietal cortex (thromboembolic).

3. Hypertension.
4. Hyperlipidemia.

HOSPITAL COURSE:
A 70-year-old male presented with acute left facial droop and left arm weakness starting 6 hours prior to arrival. Brain MRI confirmed acute non-hemorrhagic cortical infarction in the right middle cerebral artery territory. Continuous cardiac telemetry on Hospital Day 2 detected multiple asymptomatic runs of paroxysmal atrial fibrillation lasting up to 45 minutes, establishing the cardioembolic etiology of the stroke.

Neurologic deficits improved with occupational and physical therapy. Transesophageal echocardiogram revealed no left atrial appendage thrombus, and carotid duplex showed no hemodynamically significant stenosis. By Hospital Day 5, left arm motor strength had recovered to 4+/5. Systemic anticoagulation was initiated on Day 6 following a repeat non-contrast head CT confirming absence of hemorrhagic transformation.
