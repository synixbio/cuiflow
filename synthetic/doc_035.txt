REQUESTING CLINIC: Otolaryngology
REASON FOR CONSULTATION: Suspected Aspirin-Exacerbated Respiratory Disease (AERD) / Samter's Triad.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:
The patient is a 39-year-old female with adult-onset severe asthma and recurrent nasal polyposis (status-post two prior functional endoscopic sinus surgeries). She presents following an acute reaction 2 weeks ago: within 45 minutes of taking 400 mg of Ibuprofen for a headache, she developed sudden severe rhinorrhea, profound nasal congestion, conjunctival injection, followed by intense bronchospasm and wheezing requiring emergency room care and nebulizer treatments. She gives a similar history of chest tightness after taking an Alka-Seltzer 3 years ago.

CURRENT MEDICATIONS:
* Fluticasone-Salmeterol 250/50 mcg 1 puff BID
* Montelukast 10 mg PO daily
* Mometasone furoate nasal spray 2 sprays each nostril daily
* Albuterol rescue inhaler

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION:
Vitals: BP 122/74 mmHg, HR 72 bpm, SpO2 98% room air.
Nose: Anterior rhinoscopy shows marked mucosal edema and large, glistening, pale polyps filling both middle meatuses.
Chest: Clear to auscultation bilaterally, good air movement, trace end-expiratory wheeze on forced expiration.

ASSESSMENT:
Aspirin-Exacerbated Respiratory Disease (Samter's Triad) characterized by:
1. Adult-onset asthma.
2. Recurrent nasal polyposis.
3. Severe acute respiratory reactions to COX-1 inhibiting non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs.

RECOMMENDATIONS:
1. Strict avoidance of all COX-1 inhibitors (Aspirin, Ibuprofen, Naproxen, Ketorolac, Meloxicam). Acetaminophen is safe at doses <= 650 mg (weak COX inhibitor).
2. Leukotriene Modulator Optimization: Switch from Montelukast to Zileuton (5-lipoxygenase inhibitor) 600 mg QID or ER 1200 mg BID, with baseline and regular LFT monitoring.
3. Biologic Therapy: Patient is an ideal candidate for Dupilumab (anti-IL-4R alpha) 600 mg loading dose then 300 mg SQ every 2 weeks to treat both nasal polyposis and comorbid asthma. Prior authorization paperwork initiated.
4. Aspirin Desensitization: Discussed inpatient aspirin desensitization protocol if biologic therapy does not control polyp recurrence or if cardioprotective aspirin is required in the future.
