DISCHARGE DIAGNOSES:
1. Ulcerative pancolitis flare, steroid-responsive, resolving.

HOSPITAL COURSE:
A 51-year-old male with a 6-year history of ulcerative colitis maintained on Mesalamine presented with 10-12 daily bloody bowel movements, severe tenesmus, fecal urgency, and crampy lower abdominal pain for two weeks. Laboratory workup showed CRP 68 mg/L, ESR 45 mm/hr, Hemoglobin 9.4 g/dL, and negative Clostridioides difficile PCR and enteric stool pathogens. Flexible sigmoidoscopy performed on Hospital Day 2 demonstrated severe continuous erythema, granularity, deep mucosal ulcerations, and spontaneous bleeding from rectum to splenic flexure (Mayo endoscopic subscore 3).

Patient was started on IV Methylprednisolone 60 mg daily and supportive bowel rest with low-residue nutrition. Oral potassium repletion was provided. By Hospital Day 4, stool frequency decreased to 3 formed non-bloody stools daily, with resolution of urgency and normalization of CRP to 11 mg/L. Patient was transitioned to oral Prednisone 40 mg daily on Day 5 and tolerated a soft diet well. Biologic induction with Infliximab was discussed and outpatient infusion center intake was arranged.

DISCHARGE MEDICATIONS:
1. Prednisone 40 mg PO daily (taper by 5 mg per week until discontinued).
2. Mesalamine 1.2 g PO four tablets daily (4.8 g/day total).
3. Ferrous sulfate 325 mg PO daily on empty stomach with Vitamin C.
4. Calcium carbonate 500 mg / Vitamin D 400 IU PO BID during steroid course.

FOLLOW-UP PLAN:
* Outpatient gastroenterology clinic visit in 10 days for therapeutic monitoring and Infliximab biosimilar infusion scheduling.
